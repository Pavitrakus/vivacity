# Emergent Ventures Application Playbook (Deep Research)

> Compiled for Vivacity / India teen founder application prep.  
> Sources: official Mercatus form + EV pages, Tyler Cowen interviews/Q&As, published winner essays, EV India cohort posts, grantee writeups (Jehan Azad, Cedric Warny, Ari Dutilh), Swarajya / Tony Kulesa / Merge Club notes.  
> **Reality check first:** Nobody can get you selected “100%.” EV is high-trust and judgment-based. This playbook maximizes fit and kills common failure modes. Rejections are silent (no feedback). You can reapply with a stronger pitch later.

## 1. What Emergent Ventures Actually Is

| Fact | Detail |
|------|--------|
| Where | Mercatus Center, George Mason University |
| Who decides | **Tyler Cowen** reads / decides globally; **Shruti Rajagopalan** leads **EV India** (WhatsApp-call culture for India) |
| What they fund | People + high-upside “zero to one” ideas — startups, research, travel, books, olympiads, hardware, education, weird moonshots |
| Equity | **None.** Non-dilutive grant |
| Age | **13+** (you qualify at 16) |
| Geography | Global; **select “India”** if your project primarily affects India |
| Word limit | Proposal **≤ 1500 words** (must stand alone; attachments are secondary) |
| Attachments | Images/docs OK — **no PDFs** on current form |
| Speed | Form says ~1 week; India stories often: email → WhatsApp audio → money in weeks |
| After grant | Minimal reporting (often a short update later); WhatsApp grantee network; annual unconference (travel often covered) |
| Typical size | Roughly **$1k–$50k** common; outliers higher; **very small** OK if life-trajectory changing |

**Not YC.** YC = company / batch / equity. EV = bet on *you* + idea. Profit / revenue path is fine — Tyler calls quick revenue self-sufficiency “a feature not a bug.”

**India context:** EV India has funded many high-schoolers (13–18) for STEM kits, olympiad travel, career development, EdTech, drones, etc. A 16-year-old with a real product talking to Indian EdTech is *in category*, not weird.

Apply: https://mercatus.tfaforms.net/5099527

## 3. The Three-Part Proposal (Official Structure)

Tyler designed this on purpose. **Credentials are weakly weighted.** Clarity + ambition + feasibility + *you* matter.

### Part A — About you (~300–500 words)
**Prompt:** Personal story linked to what you want to do. Brief background. Awards only if germane.

**What winners do:**
- Story of *growing ambition* (Jehan Azad: motivations > credentials)
- Concrete things you *did* (built X, emailed Y, rendered Z)
- Time pressure / constraints that make the grant matter (school + build, India compute cost, etc.)
- Cedric Warny: short arc “who I am → why this problem stuck”

**What fails:**
- Resume bullet dump
- “Passionate about leveraging synergies…”
- Age as the whole pitch (“I’m 16 so fund me”) — age is context, not the product

**Vivacity-ready signals (when we draft):**
- Built working Manim → TTS → stitch pipeline
- On-device quality / real latency numbers you’ll stand behind
- Conversations with ProLearn, JEE Simplified; PW CTO replied asking for walkthrough
- Why *JEE / Indian student chatbot* is personal (need your real story — you fill this)

### Part B — One mainstream / consensus view you agree with (~80–200 words)
**Prompt:** “What is one mainstream or consensus view that you absolutely agree with?”  
*(Their “trick” reversing fashionable contrarianism.)*

**What works (from winners + commentary):**
- Something **true that smart people nod at**, not edge-lord bait
- Optionally: consensus in *practice* that people are embarrassed to say aloud (Jehan: “Amazon is great” → then a sharp economic reason tied to his project)
- Cedric: education externalities + rule of law as wealth — calm, sourced, relevant to governance course

**What fails:**
- Fake hot takes (“school is obsolete”) when you still need school
- Long philosophy essay
- Agreeing with something vaguely fashionable but empty
- Using this section to dunk on rivals

**Good shape:**  
1 sentence claim → 2–4 sentences why → optional 1 sentence how it connects to your project (light touch)

### Part C — The idea (~500–800 words)
**Prompt:** Convince it’s worth investing in. What’s new/unusual. What’s the problem.

**What winners do (Jehan pattern is gold):**
1. **Problem** — specific, real, measurable pain  
2. **Why existing paths fail** — short, not rant  
3. **What you’re building** — concrete mechanism  
4. **Proof you already started** — demos, nodes live, users, letters, cost numbers  
5. **What’s new** — not “AI is new”; your wedge  
6. **What the money buys** — clear next 3–6 months  
7. **Status** — full-time / part-time, how long, partners

**Tyler’s stated rejection modes (Mercatus Q&A):**
- Ambitious but **not feasible / under-specified**
- Smart but **crowded / over-capitalized** area
- **Didn’t follow instructions**

**Also common:** timidness; no strange or sharp idea (Tyler AEA note: common mistake = timidness)

**For Vivacity — avoid the trap:**  
Don’t frame as “ChatGPT wrapper for Manim” or “Indian Lamina.”  
Frame as: *personalized precise math/physics animation for Indian learners & EdTech (live, multilingual), where human Manim costs ₹thousands/hour of output* — with **honest** latency/cost, and path to school/creator/chatbot distribution.

## 5. Microgrant Mentality (How Small Asks Win)

Tyler explicitly: *very small grants OK if they change trajectory.*

**Strong micro/small asks (~$3k–$15k) look like:**
| Line | Why |
|------|-----|
| Cloud GPU / render box for N months | Stops “only works on my laptop” |
| API spend (Claude/OpenAI) for pilots | Real cost of demos for ProLearn/PW |
| Travel (Bengaluru / Delhi) for walkthroughs | Converts email interest → meetings |
| 1 short contractor / intern | Captioning, QA, Hindi scripts |
| Domain + basic infra | Credibility |

**Weak asks:**
- Vague “marketing”
- “Living stipend” with no build plan (sometimes OK for career grants — but you’re a product founder; tie money to product)
- Asking like a Series A ($100k+) with no team

**Write budget as:**  
`Total asked: $X`  
`Render/cloud: $A`  
`Model/API: $B`  
`Travel/demos: $C`  
`Misc: $D`  
Optional: “Family covers living; grant is almost 100% product.”

## 7. Sounding Human (Not AI)

Tyler/Shruti read tons of essays. Generic LLM paste is a silent killer — not because “AI bad,” because it sounds like **nobody specific**.

### Kill these AI tells
- “In today’s rapidly evolving world…”
- “leverage / utilize / delve / tapestry / landscape / furthermore / moreover”
- Perfect parallel bullet sermons with emojis
- Fake humility + fake boldness in the same paragraph
- Over-explaining what Manim is as if to a child, then claiming rocket science
- Lists of 7 benefits ending in “and more”
- Triple em-dash dramatic prose everywhere

### Sound like a 16-year-old builder who ships
- Short sentences. Occasional imperfect rhythm.
- Specific numbers you can defend (minutes to render, ₹ cost vs freelancer, languages).
- Name real places/people *carefully* (ProLearn interest, JEE Simplified talks, PW CTO reply asking for walkthrough — **not** “partnership with PW”).
- Admit limits: “On my machine a 2-min clip takes ~3–4 min. Cloud is what I’m stuck on.”
- One personal image: a class you hated, a YouTube channel that hooked you, nights debugging LaTeX — **true**, not invented.

### Drafting process that stays human
1. You dictate / write messy first pass in your own words (voice notes → text OK)
2. Structure into A/B/C sections
3. Cut to ≤1500, strip adjectives
4. Read aloud once — if you wouldn’t say it to Shruti on WhatsApp, delete it
5. Optional AI for *tightening only* after your voice is there — then rewrite 30% by hand again

### Interview voice (if you get the WhatsApp call)
Ari Dutilh (EV India grantee) TL;DR: know **what / why / spend**. Be yourself. Short call. Enjoy it.  
Don’t recite the essay. Have 3 demo links open. Know your unit cost. Know next milestone.

Prep watching/reading (Ari’s list + basics):
- Emergent Ventures Q&A / Tyler on Talent
- Tyler + Shruti “Geopolitics of Talent”
- Shruti on Indian economy (shows you respect the India lens)
- Tony Kulesa: “Tyler Cowen is the best curator of talent…”

## 8. Honesty Rules That Prevent Blowups

| Claim in essay | Safe version |
|----------------|--------------|
| “90 seconds always” | Only if you’ll demo that live; else use your real 3–4 min |
| “Partnership with PW” | “CTO replied and asked for a detailed product walkthrough” |
| “Almost free forever” | Unit economics now + path once render is centralized |
| “Better than all AI video” | “Better for *precise* math animation than whiteboard tools; different job than Lamina/Simi” |
| “YC-ready company” | Don’t. EV ≠ YC. Ambition yes; cosplay no |

Exaggeration is how you lose a trust-based grant.

## 9. Competitive Positioning (For Clarity, Not Trash Talk)

You may get “isn’t this crowded AI video?”

Answer shape:
- Consumer cinematic AI video ≠ teaching math correctly
- Whiteboard generators optimize speed of doodles
- Manim is what serious math/physics explainers already use (3B1B tradition) — expensive human labor in India EdTech (~hours + ₹thousands)
- Your wedge: **live generation + multilingual narration + India distribution (creators / bots / schools)**

Mention competitors only if needed. Spend words on *your* users’ pain.

## 11. After Submit

1. Expect silence or email within ~1–2 weeks (form says ~1 week; don’t panic at 10 days)
2. If India: prepare for **Shruti WhatsApp audio** — quiet room, demos ready
3. If “tell us more on revenue”: have a one-pager: who pays (EdTech B2B vs creator self-serve), price sketch, pilot shape
4. If no: reapply later with stronger pilot proof — Tyler has explicitly invited reapplications
5. Tax/logistics: grant may be taxable; parent helps receiving as minor — ask Mercatus ops if awarded
